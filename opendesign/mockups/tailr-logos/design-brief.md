# Tailr — Logo Design Brief

## About the Product

Tailr is an open-source tool that tailors your LaTeX resume to specific job descriptions using LLMs. It preserves exact formatting while letting AI make targeted, reviewable edits. The UI uses proofreading marks (green additions, red deletions) on the rendered document.

## Brand Vibe

Open-source tech. Think Vite, Zod, Drizzle — short names, clean marks, minimal color, maximum recognizability. Not corporate. Not playful. Precise and quietly confident.

## Typography

**Wordmark: Space Grotesk, 700 weight, lowercase.**
- `tailr` — not `Tailr` in the logo lockup
- Letter-spacing: `-0.02em` (slightly tight)
- The "t" and "l" being ascenders gives it a subtle vertical rhythm that echoes the document/page metaphor

## Core Metaphor

A **document page with a diagonal cut**.

The cut is the action: tailoring, editing, proofreading. The colored edge that shows through is the signature brand moment. Like a tailor's notch on fabric — it reveals that something precise has happened.

The paper should feel physical: subtle warm gradient, soft drop shadow, faint content lines suggesting a resume (but abstract enough to read as "document" at small sizes).

## Variation 1: Classic Redline

**Concept:** The purest form. A single document page with a clean diagonal cut from lower-left to upper-right.

**Visual description:**
- Rounded rectangle (paper), slightly taller than wide, warm white gradient top-to-bottom
- 8-10 faint horizontal lines inside, varying lengths, suggesting resume text but abstract
- A bold diagonal line cuts from bottom-left edge to top-right edge at roughly 55 degrees
- Behind the cut line: a colored edge fill in the primary blue gradient (light to dark), creating the illusion of paper thickness or a fresh cut surface
- A thin bevel highlight line running parallel to the cut, slightly offset, in the light accent color — this sells the "fresh cut" feel
- Soft drop shadow behind the entire paper shape, giving it lift off the background
- Corner fold detail: tiny dog-ear at the top-right, subtly suggesting a physical page

**Mood:** Minimal, iconic, works at 16px. This is the default mark.

---

## Variation 2: Scissors Cut

**Concept:** Scissors in mid-action, cutting through the document along the same diagonal line.

**Visual description:**
- Same paper shape and content lines as Variation 1
- The diagonal cut line is present but slightly muted (thinner, lower opacity red)
- A pair of tailor's scissors positioned so the blades follow the diagonal cut line
- **Top blade:** Metallic silver gradient, entering from upper-left, crossing the page, exiting lower-right. Slight curve to suggest open scissors. Blade shine/highlight along the cutting edge.
- **Bottom blade:** Slightly darker metallic, crossing underneath, entering from lower-left, exiting upper-right
- **Pivot screw:** Small circle where blades cross, dark gray with a specular highlight
- **Handles:** Brand blue gradient (light to dark), finger loops, one handle extending below-left, one above-right. Handles should read as circles/ovals at distance but have slight ergonomic shape up close
- The scissors should look like they're *currently cutting* — blades are open, not closed
- A tiny paper curl or slight lift at the point where the cut starts (lower-left edge), suggesting the paper is being cut in real time

**Mood:** Action, precision, dynamic. More detailed than Classic — better for larger applications.

---

## Variation 3: Diagonal Stitch

**Concept:** Thread sewn diagonally across the page through stitch holes. The thread IS the cut line.

**Visual description:**
- Same paper shape and content lines
- A series of evenly spaced small circles (stitch holes) running diagonally from bottom-left to top-right, about 6-7 holes total
- Holes have thin borders in a warm gray, empty centers showing paper color through
- **Thread segments** connecting each hole: thick (2.5px) line in primary blue, slightly curved between each hole — not perfectly straight, giving it a hand-sewn feel
- **Needle** at the bottom-left end: a thin metallic shaft extending below the paper, with a small blue circle (thread knot) where it meets the first hole
- **Thread tail** at the top-right end: a short segment of thread extending past the last hole, ending in a small blue dot or a tiny loop suggesting it was just pulled through
- Subtle thread shadow: a very thin, dark line offset slightly below each thread segment, giving the thread physical presence (like it sits on top of the paper)

**Mood:** Craft, tactile, artisanal. Appeals to the "precision editing" story without being aggressive like scissors.

---

## Variation 4: Layered Cut

**Concept:** Three stacked pages, each slightly offset and rotated, with the diagonal cut going through all layers. Reveals colored edges at three depths.

**Visual description:**
- Three identical paper rectangles stacked:
  - **Back layer:** darkest paper color, rotated -2 degrees, offset down-right by ~8px
  - **Middle layer:** mid-tone paper, rotated -1 degree, offset down-right by ~4px
  - **Front layer:** lightest paper, straight, soft drop shadow
- All three layers have the same content line pattern, but lines are progressively darker/more visible on front layers
- The diagonal cut goes through all three layers identically
- At the cut edge: three visible colored strips stacked, each slightly offset, creating a stepped edge effect
- The front layer edge is the primary blue. The middle and back layer edges use progressively darker blues, creating a canyon/rainbow edge effect
- A single bevel highlight on the front-most cut edge
- Each layer has its own subtle shadow, adding to the depth

**Mood:** Rich, dimensional, layered. Suggests version history, multiple drafts, depth of work. Best for product screenshots and hero imagery.

---

## Variation 5: Scissors Action

**Concept:** Scissors in full wide-open position, framing the document. The most dynamic composition.

**Visual description:**
- Paper centered, standard shape and content
- Cut line present but very thin/subtle — almost a guide line
- **Scissors:** Blades fully open and crossed in an X shape over the page
  - Top blade: upper-left to lower-right, metallic gradient with highlight
  - Bottom blade: lower-left to upper-right, slightly darker
  - Blades cross near the center of the page, not at the geometric center but slightly offset for visual interest
  - **Pivot screw** at the crossing point, detailed with a dark center and metallic rim
  - **Handles:** One extends far to the lower-left (brand blue), one to the upper-right (dark blue). They're large, open loops, not closed yet
- The handles frame the document — they reach outside the paper bounds, making the mark feel bigger and more assertive
- Blade shine/reflection: a thin white highlight stroke along each blade's cutting edge
- Paper slightly distorted at the cut point: a tiny ripple or curl where the blades meet the paper, suggesting pressure

**Mood:** Bold, confident, editorial. This is the marketing mark — for Twitter headers, launch graphics, README hero.

---

## Variation 6: Thread & Needle

**Concept:** The most narrative version. A complete sewing scene: needle with thread knot, curved stitch segments, spool tail, all on the page.

**Visual description:**
- Same paper base with content lines
- **Stitch holes:** 6-7 circles running diagonal, with the warm gray borders
- **Thread segments:** Curved between holes in primary blue, thicker than Variation 3. Each segment has a slight S-curve, not just a single arc. A thin dark shadow offset beneath each segment
- **Needle:** Positioned below-left of the paper. Detailed: thin metallic shaft, small eye (ellipse) near the top, the thread passing through the eye and knotting below. The needle enters the first stitch hole from below
- **Thread knot:** A small blue circle/blob where the thread emerges from the needle eye, sitting just below the paper edge
- **Thread end:** After the last stitch hole (top-right), the thread extends upward and curves left in a loose loop, ending in a small blue bead/knot — like excess thread after a stitch
- **Optional spool:** A very small blue cylinder shape at the end of the thread tail, suggesting the thread came from somewhere
- The thread should feel continuous: one unbroken line from needle → through all holes → to the tail end

**Mood:** Story-rich, warm, human. Best for community/blog contexts. Slightly more decorative than the others.

## Scaling Requirements

The mark must work at three sizes:

| Size | Context | Details that must survive |
|---|---|---|
| **48×48px** | App icon, product hunt thumbnail | Paper shape, 2-3 content lines, cut line visible, colored edge hint |
| **32×32px** | Favicon, browser tab | Paper shape recognizable, cut line visible, content lines reduced to 2-3 abstract dashes |
| **16×16px** | Tiny inline mark | Paper rectangle + diagonal cut line + colored edge triangle. Minimal, but still readable as "document with cut" |

At 16px, the mark can drop: content lines, shadows, bevel highlights. Keep: paper shape, cut line, colored edge.

---

## Wordmark Lockups

Three approved lockup configurations:

1. **Mark + Wordmark (horizontal)** — Mark left, "tailr" right. Gap: ~8px. Baseline of wordmark aligns with vertical center of mark.
2. **Stacked (vertical)** — Mark above, "tailr" below. Gap: ~12px. Centered. Better for square contexts (avatars, app icons with text).
3. **Wordmark alone** — "tailr" in Space Grotesk 700. No mark. Used when space is constrained or the mark is already present nearby.

---

## Background Treatments

| Context | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Dark backgrounds (primary) | Paper in warm white, all colored elements in primary blue family. Drop shadow essential for lift. |
| Light backgrounds | Same mark, but paper shadow becomes softer and lighter. Blue elements may need to shift to slightly darker values for contrast. |
| Accent/blue backgrounds | Paper in pure white (no warm tint). Colored edge uses white or very light blue. This is the bold favicon treatment. |
| Monochrome | All colored elements become the same value as the text. Cut line becomes a thick stroke. Works for single-color printing. |
